Command Deck

Where the money and the risk are today. Ashish opens this Monday morning; Poonam's daily list lives in §11.

North Star

Contribution margin per day — pieces dispatched × (price − COGM − channel cost), averaged over the week. Revenue is vanity in garments; a lakh of Meesho sales at 25% returns can lose money while ₹30k of D2C prints cash. Secondary star: return rate under 15% — it's the single number that says the product, photos and QC are all working.

Festival radar — production clocks, computed live

Garments are a calendar business: whoever cuts fabric on time owns the festival. Production start = peak minus lead time; catalogs must be live 3–4 weeks before peak for marketplaces to pick them up.

Standing alert rules

  • Critical · 24 hrs  Any style's return rate crosses 30% → pause the listing, inspect returned pieces, fix photos/size-chart/QC before relisting.
  • Critical · 24 hrs  Job worker 7+ days past deadline → physical visit today, fabric recovery plan agreed in writing.
  • Critical · 24 hrs  Cash reserve below ₹25,000 → freeze fabric buying, collect wholesale dues, emergency plan with §9 ladder.
  • Warning · 3 days  Hero SKU stock under 20 pieces → cutting order + job-work order now; heroes never go out of stock.
  • Warning · 3 days  Shade variation found after cutting starts → stop the line, photograph, call the fabric supplier the same day.
  • Opportunity  Stock older than 45 days → move it: bundle offer on WhatsApp, exhibition rack, or reseller push. Dead stock is frozen cash.

Costing Engine

Cost every style before cutting fabric, price it per channel from one COGM, and never let a listing slip under the floor. Inputs save on this device.

Style cost — type the real numbers

Fabric is 55–70% of COGM — a ₹5/metre negotiation moves the whole P&L more than any other line. Overhead and rejection are percentages of the base cost; keep rejection honest or every "cheap" batch lies to you.

Channel price ladder — from this COGM

Price = COGM × channel multiplier, nudged to a 9-ending. GST band and margin follow the price. The floor row is the never-cross line.

Channel×PriceGST bandGross marginHealth

Margin per piece by channel

Price minus COGM minus typical channel cost (commission/fees at the rates in §7). Hover any bar for the working.

Cost reduction levers — pull in this order

  1. Fabric rate: second supplier per fabric type + 100m/500m slab discounts (10–15%) + Bhilwara/Sanganer direct visits.
  2. Marker efficiency: 85% → 90% marker = ~5% fabric saved. Track wastage per cutting in the Baisa OS cutting register; target ≤ 8%.
  3. Rejection: every 1% off rejection is 1% off COGM — QC gate (§6) pays for itself here.
  4. Job-work rate: steady 500+ pcs/month volume earns 5–10% below market; pay on time, negotiate yearly.
  5. Packaging buying: polybags and tags at 5,000+ quantity drop 20–30%.
  6. Batch size: minimum 50 pcs per style-colour — under that, setup time eats the margin.

Costing discipline

  • No cost sheet, no cutting. Every new style gets this engine run and the result saved into the Baisa OS costing module before fabric is touched.
  • Re-cost quarterly: fabric and job-work rates drift; a style costed in March is lying by September.
  • Landed COGM includes packing — the branded polybag, tag and thank-you card are product cost, not marketing.
  • Wholesale floor is sacred: COGM × 2.0. A boutique pushing below floor is offered volume terms, not a lower price.

Section 5

Factory Floor

Production is the moat. This is the flow, the capacity maths, the fabric game and the job-work rules.

The production bible — never skip a step

Design→ Fabric sourcing→ Pattern + grading→ Sample → approval→ Cost sheet (§3)→ Fabric in + inspection→ Marker + cutting→ Stitching→ Finishing→ In-line QC→ Final QC (§6)→ Pack→ Dispatch

Every skipped step comes back as a return, a complaint or a re-cut. The tech pack is the contract for every run: measurements per size (±0.5 cm), fabric spec (type/GSM/colour code), stitch type + SPI, thread, trims, label placement, packing instructions. No tech pack, no job-work handover.

Capacity maths

Capacity = operators × efficiency% × minutes ÷ SAM. Plan festival volumes with this, not hope.

GarmentSAM (min)Pcs/operator/day*
Palazzo10–14~28
Basic kurti12–18~22
Printed kurti20–28~14

Co-ord set28–38~10

*at 70% efficiency, 480-min day. A 5-tailor line ≈ 50–110 pcs/day depending on style — which is why co-ord-heavy festival plans need job-work booked 8 weeks out.

Machine discipline

  • Oil every machine every morning; needles changed every 8–10 running hours (skipped stitches = blunt needle, not bad tailor).
  • Spare kit on premises: needles 9/11/14/16, bobbins, belts, presser feet.
  • Full service every 3 months (~₹500–1,000/machine) — cheaper than one seized machine in Navratri week.

Buying rules

  • Kurti ≈ 2.5–2.8m of 44" — always order +10% over calculated consumption.
  • Single dye lot per style-colour; approve a shade band before cutting. Pre-wash cotton (shrinkage <3%).
  • Test before buying: rub test, water-dip bleed test, crease-recovery, pull test on knits.
  • Second visit buys, not the first. 100m+ demands 10–15% off + delivery; 500m+ pushes Net-30 credit.
  • Bank payment only for big lots — the GST ITC is real margin.

Job-work network — scale without buying machines

The five golden rules

  1. Never all fabric at once — 100 pcs worth, receive, then next batch.
  2. Deadline in writing — a WhatsApp message is a legal record; date every handover.
  3. Rejection clause agreed upfront — bad stitching is redone at contractor cost.
  4. Max 30% advance, balance on delivery after QC.
  5. 2–3 contractors per work type — single-source is how festivals get missed.

Evaluating a new unit

Visit physically · count machines & condition · sample on your fabric · ask who else they stitch for · rate per piece (never per hour) · GST-registered for ITC · delivery date + rejection terms on WhatsApp before fabric moves.

Quality Gate

Returns are the tax on sloppy QC. Three gates, checkable per batch — run them and the return rate falls on its own.

Gate 2 · In-line (10% sampling)

Gate 3 · Final before packing

AQL rule & defect fixes

Sample 10% of every batch. Defects <2.5% → accept · 2.5–6.5% → hold and 100% check · >6.5% → reject the batch back to the line/contractor.

DefectCause → fix
Puckered seamsThread tension → adjust upper tension
Skipped stitchesBlunt/wrong needle → change immediately
Colour bleedingPoor dye → pre-wash all fabric before cutting
Uneven hemCutter error → retrain, use guide
Collar asymmetryNo jig → make a cardboard collar jig

Return rate — the verdict metric

RateVerdictAction
<15%excellentScale the style
15–20%acceptableMonitor weekly
20–25%warningAudit photos + size chart + QC
>25%criticalPause listing, root-cause, fix, relist

Reason → fix: "size wrong" → measure real garments into the chart (not standard charts) · "colour different" → daylight photos, no filters · "quality" → Gate 2/3 failure, trace the batch · "not as shown" → rewrite the listing.
Return SOP: photograph before opening → resalable? refold + relist · minor defect? fix in-house · damaged? platform claim within 72 hrs → write-off feeds the rejection % in §3.

Money

Garment cash flow: pay fabric today, get platform money in 7–21 days. The gap is working capital — manage it like a hawk.

Weekly cash dashboard — Friday, with the bank app open

In this weekMeesho settlement (T+7 of dispatch) · Amazon/Flipkart (T+14) · D2C payouts (T+2) · wholesale dues collected · exhibition cash
Out this weekFabric payments · job-work bills · wages/salaries · courier & platform fees · rent, electricity, packaging
Net positionIn − Out. Two red Fridays in a row = stop fabric buying and collect.
Safety floor2 weeks of operating expenses in the account, always. Below ₹25k reserve = §1 critical alert.

The four cash rules

  1. Never buy fabric beyond 30 days of production capacity.
  2. Wholesale terms are Net-15, not Net-30. Interest-free credit is a gift we don't give.
  3. Emergency fund ₹25–50k untouched in the business account.
  4. Business account ≠ personal account. Mixing = flying blind.
